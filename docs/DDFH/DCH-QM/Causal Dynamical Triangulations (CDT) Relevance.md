Core Concept

Causal Dynamical Triangulations (CDT) is a quantum gravity approach that models spacetime as an emergent structure built from discrete, simplicial building blocks (triangles, tetrahedra) while preserving causal structure. CDT demonstrates that spacetime dimensionality is not static but dynamically scales with energy and confinement. Within the Deterministic Dimensional Flow Hypothesis (DDFH), CDT provides a critical foundation by supporting the idea that dimensional accessibility is not universal, but scale-dependent—a cornerstone of explaining quantum phenomena as projection artifacts of dimensionally constrained geometric flows.

**Explanation**

CDT shows that at Planck-scale energies, spacetime behaves effectively as a 2D structure, while at macroscopic scales, it unfolds into the familiar 3+1D spacetime.

DDFH extends this concept by proposing that quantum behaviors arise from interacting directly with these lower-dimensional configurations, where dimensional pathways are compactified or inaccessible.

The projection of quantum systems into 3D observational frameworks introduces probabilistic artifacts, much like CDT’s lower-dimensional simplices aggregate into higher-dimensional emergent geometry.


---


**Implications**

  
1. Dimensional accessibility is scale-dependent, not fixed—a principle shared by both CDT and DDFH.

2. Quantum phenomena emerge from interactions at scales where spacetime’s effective dimensionality is reduced.

3. The classical world emerges through geometric aggregation (triangulation) as dimensional accessibility unfolds.
