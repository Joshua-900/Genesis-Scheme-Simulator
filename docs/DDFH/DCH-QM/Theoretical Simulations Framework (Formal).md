
## Objective

To develop computational simulations that model how dimensional constraints influence quantum systems, thereby testing the Deterministic Dimensional Flow Hypothesis (DDFH). These simulations aim to show how quantum probabilistic behaviors emerge from projection artifacts and predict scenarios where deviations from standard QM may appear.

## Modeling Approach

### 1. Dimensional Constraint Modeling

- Simulate canonical QM systems (particle-in-a-box, harmonic oscillator, double-slit).
- Impose relational constraints (geometric folding, compactification).
- Track wavefunction deformation, uncertainty scaling, and interference pattern shifts.

### 2. Projection Artifact Simulation

- Collapse hidden variables into observable amplitudes via projection operators.
- Observe emergence of probabilistic outcomes as artifacts of dimensional overfitting.
- Compare superposition/entanglement patterns against projection-driven simplifications.

### 3. Scale-Dependent Dimensional Unfolding

- Model unfolding of dimensional accessibility with system size.
- Explore mesoscopic regimes for hybrid behaviors between QM and classical.
- Visualize transition of relational adjacency into spatial orthogonality.

## Computational Techniques

- Finite Element Analysis (FEA) for spatial confinement.
- Lattice-based simulations for constrained flows.
- Differential geometry algorithms for folding/unfolding manifolds.
- Stochastic differential equations as placeholders for inaccessible relational variables.

## Validation Strategy

- Compare outputs to known quantum results:
    - Energy levels under confinement.
    - Bell-test correlations modeled via adjacency.
    - Interference deformations under constraints.
- Identify deviations as **predictive markers** for experimental falsification.
