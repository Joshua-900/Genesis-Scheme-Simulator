
## Definition

Within **DDFH**, Bell’s Theorem is re-read as a constraint on **3D spatial locality**, not on **determinism** per se. Entanglement correlations that violate Bell inequalities indicate that 3D locality is an insufficient modeling assumption. In DDFH, outcomes are **deterministically coordinated** by **geometric adjacency** on a hidden substrate (Σ); the appearance of “non-locality” in 3D bulk (B) is a **projection artifact**. Operational no‑signaling is preserved.

---

## Core Concept

- **3D locality fails; Σ‑locality holds:** Causality is local on the substrate Σ even when the bulk B shows spacelike separation.
- **Deterministic adjacency:** Entangled systems remain adjacent in Σ (compactified geometry), so correlations are causal in Σ though non-local in B.
- **Projection consistency:** No-signaling in B emerges from consistent projection of Σ‑local dynamics.

---

## Problem Statement

- Does Bell’s Theorem invalidate determinism?
- Can Bell inequality violations be explained by geometric (substrate) locality rather than by ontological randomness or superluminal influence?

---

## Explanation

- **Bell’s Theorem** rules out **local hidden variables in 3D spacetime** under standard assumptions (locality/factorizability, measurement independence, and realism).
- **DDFH move:** Relax _3D locality_; retain determinism, measurement independence, and operational no‑signaling by relocating causal adjacency to Σ.
- Apparent “instantaneous” correlations arise because **d_Σ(ι(x_A), ι(x_B)) ≈ 0** even when **‖x_A − x_B‖** is large in B.

## Implications

1. Bell violations do **not** refute determinism; they refute **3D local** hidden variables.
2. Entanglement correlations are **Σ‑local** and deterministic; their 3D “non‑local” look is a projection artifact.
3. No‑signaling is preserved as a property of the projection , not as fundamental dynamical superluminality.
