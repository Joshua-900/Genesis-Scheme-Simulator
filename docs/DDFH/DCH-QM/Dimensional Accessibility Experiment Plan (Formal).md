
## Objective

To design experimental approaches that investigate whether dimensional accessibility dynamically scales with physical confinement and energy levels, thereby testing the Deterministic Dimensional Flow Hypothesis (DDFH).

---

## Hypothesis

If dimensional accessibility is scale-dependent:

1. Strong confinement alters quantum behaviors.
2. Quantum-classical transitions correlate with unfolding thresholds.

**Symbolic Sketch:**

```latex

\alpha(s) : \text{scale} \to D_{eff}, \quad  
\lim_{s \to 0} \alpha(s) = D_{substrate}, \quad  
\lim_{s \to \infty} \alpha(s) = D_{bulk}
```

---

## Experimental Proposals

### 1. Quantum Confinement in Nanostructures

- **Setup:** Quantum wells, dots, and graphene sheets to confine electrons.
- **Prediction:** Extreme confinement reduces uncertainties, revealing deterministic patterns.
- **Measurements:**
    - Position/momentum uncertainties vs confinement.
    - Energy discretization as 2D limits are approached.

### 2. Entanglement Scaling in Constrained Geometries

- **Setup:** Entangle photons/electrons in nanoscale waveguides or photonic crystals.
- **Prediction:** Correlation strength varies with imposed relational adjacency.
- **Measurements:**
    - Bell test outcomes across confinement regimes.
    - Correlation vs geometric proximity.

### 3. Decoherence Threshold Mapping

- **Setup:** Systems from mesoscopic to macroscopic scales under isolation.
- **Prediction:** Decoherence transitions track dimensional unfolding thresholds, not just environment.
- **Measurements:**
    - Identify scale points where probabilism → determinism.
