## Objective

To develop computational simulations that model how dimensional constraints influence quantum systems, thereby testing the Deterministic Dimensional Flow Hypothesis (DDFH). These simulations aim to demonstrate how quantum probabilistic behaviors arise from projection-induced relational limitations and predict scenarios where deviations from standard quantum mechanical models may emerge.

---

## Modeling Approach

### 1. **Dimensional Constraint Modeling**

- Simulate quantum systems (particle-in-a-box, harmonic oscillator, double-slit interference) with dynamically adjustable degrees of spatial freedom.
- Introduce **relational constraints** by progressively limiting accessible dimensions, mimicking geometric folding or compactification effects.
- Monitor how wavefunctions deform, uncertainties scale, and interference patterns shift under these constraints.

### 2. **Projection Artifact Simulation**

- Implement projection algorithms that collapse hidden relational variables into observable amplitudes.
- Analyze how probabilistic outcomes emerge as an artifact of modeling dimensionally constrained systems with an overfitted spatial framework.
- Explore whether certain quantum anomalies (e.g., superposition, entanglement behavior) align with projection-induced simplifications.

### 3. **Scale-Dependent Dimensional Unfolding**

- Simulate scaling transitions where dimensional accessibility unfolds with system size.
- Model mesoscopic systems to observe transitional behaviors between quantum probabilistic models and classical determinism.
- Visualize how relational pathways unfold into spatial orthogonality as scale increases.

---

## Computational Techniques

- Finite Element Analysis (FEA) for modeling spatial confinement effects.
- Lattice-based simulations for constrained geometric flows.
- Differential geometry algorithms to model folding/unfolding dynamics of compactified manifolds.
- Stochastic differential equations (as placeholders) to highlight where probabilistic modeling compensates for missing relational data.

---

## Validation Strategy

- Compare simulation outputs to existing quantum experimental data:
    - Energy level shifts in confined quantum systems.
    - Bell-test entanglement correlations modeled through geometric adjacency.
    - Interference pattern deformations under constrained projections.
- Identify deviations or patterns where DDFH predictions diverge from standard QM expectations, creating testable experimental hypotheses.

---

## Toolkits & Resources

- Numerical Simulation Platforms: MATLAB, COMSOL Multiphysics, Python-based custom simulation environments.
- Visualization Tools: 3D relational graph visualization to illustrate unfolding dimensional structures.
- High-performance computing resources for simulating large-scale mesoscopic systems.
