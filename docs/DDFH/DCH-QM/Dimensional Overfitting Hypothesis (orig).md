
## Core Concept

Quantum mechanics (QM) universally applies a 3D spatial model, assuming full dimensional accessibility across all scales. The Dimensional Overfitting Hypothesis asserts that this assumption is flawed at quantum scales, where systems may be relationally constrained to lower-dimensional manifolds (2D or fractional-D). Applying a 3D model to such systems introduces artificial degrees of freedom, forcing probabilistic formalisms to compensate for geometric inaccessibility.

---

## Problem Statement

- **Standard QM models solve for degrees of freedom that may not physically exist in constrained quantum systems.**
- **Probability emerges not because of inherent randomness, but because the model is “solving for variables that are dimensionally absent.”**
- This overfitting leads to:
    - Superposition interpreted as multi-state existence rather than relational ambiguity.
    - Probability clouds where deterministic pathways exist but are dimensionally inaccessible.
    - Non-locality perceived as instantaneous action rather than geometric adjacency in hidden dimensions.

---

## Implications

1. **Quantum uncertainty is a projection artifact of dimensional over-assumption.**
2. **Wavefunction collapse is a model correction event**—realigning the overfitted model back to observable constraints.
3. **Dimensional reduction at small scales naturally explains why probabilistic models are necessary without invoking fundamental indeterminacy.**
4. **Quantum phenomena emerge from a mismatch between model dimensionality and system relational structure.**

---

## Supporting Analogies

- **Epicycles in Ptolemaic Astronomy:** Correctly predicted planetary positions but relied on flawed geocentric assumptions—overfitting circular orbits to elliptical paths.
- **2D Shadow of a 3D Object:** Attempting to describe depth within a shadow by adding probabilistic fuzziness rather than acknowledging dimensional loss.

## Path Forward

- Develop mathematical models that **reduce degrees of freedom dynamically with scale (Dimensional Accessibility Functions).**
- Recalculate basic QM systems (particle-in-a-box, hydrogen atom) under constrained dimensional assumptions to observe whether probabilistic formalisms reduce or vanish.
- Explore if existing quantum anomalies (decoherence thresholds, tunneling effects) align with predicted dimensional transition points.

---

## Summary Statement

> “Dimensional Overfitting forces quantum models to invoke probability as a compensatory measure. By aligning model dimensionality with the system’s true relational structure, apparent quantum randomness may resolve into deterministic geometry.”
