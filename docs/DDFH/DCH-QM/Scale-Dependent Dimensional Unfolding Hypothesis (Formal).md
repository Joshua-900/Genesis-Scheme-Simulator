## Definition

The **Scale-Dependent Dimensional Unfolding Hypothesis (SDDUH)** extends the Deterministic Dimensional Flow Hypothesis (DDFH) by proposing that dimensional accessibility is a dynamic, scale-dependent property of reality. At quantum scales, relational pathways are folded into constrained geometries, creating hidden adjacencies that manifest as non-local correlations. As systems scale up, these folds progressively unfold, restoring orthogonality and classical locality.

---

## Problem Statement

- Why do quantum systems exhibit non-locality while macroscopic systems obey classical locality?
- Can this transition be explained as a continuous geometric unfolding rather than a mysterious quantum-classical divide?

---

## Explanation

- At small scales, dimensional constraints cause relational pathways to fold, bringing seemingly distant elements into hidden adjacency.
- As systems scale up (energy dissipation, decoherence), these folds relax, allowing relational pathways to express fuller dimensional orthogonality.
- Quantum non-locality is a scale-bound projection artifact—a glimpse into reality before spatial dimensionality fully unfolds.
- Classical locality is not fundamental; it emerges from dimensional unfolding with scale.

---

## Implications

1. Non-locality is a confined-scale phenomenon; locality emerges through dimensional relaxation.
2. Quantum-classical transitions reflect a continuous geometric unfolding, not a discrete ontological split.
3. Mesoscopic systems may display hybrid behaviors, providing testable predictions.
4. Cosmic-scale topology may reflect large-scale dimensional unfolding dynamics.

## Supporting Analogies

- **Origami Unfolding:** Compact folds hide adjacency; unfolding reveals separation.
- **Coiled Spring Stretching:** Compressed = adjacency; stretched = true spacing revealed.
