### In a nutshell

* **Planck scale** in DDFH is the *microscopic cut‑off* where the 2‑D hidden substrate starts to “talk” to the emergent 3‑D bulk.  
* **Classicality** is not tied to that microscopic scale; it is the *large‑τ* or *coarse‑grained* limit in which the hidden field has been smoothed out so that the projection rule yields deterministic outcomes and the metric satisfies the Einstein equations.

In short, the Planck scale marks the **beginning** of the dimensional‑flow process, while the quantum‑to‑classical crossover is the **end** of the process.  They are related but not identical.

## 1.  The two “critical” scales in DDFH

| Scale | What it is | Where it shows up in the equations | Physical meaning |
|-------|------------|------------------------------------|-------------------|
| **Planck length \(l_{\mathrm P}\)** | The fundamental length that limits the spectrum of the hidden field on \(T^{2}\). | The hidden‑field action \(V[\mathcal Q]\) contains a UV cut‑off \(k_{\max}\sim 1/l_{\mathrm P}\); the coupling term \(J_g\) in the metric flow is significant only for modes with wavelength \(\lesssim l_{\mathrm P}\). | **Microscopic coupling** – below this length the 2‑D substrate is “quantum‑like”; above it the bulk behaves classically. |
| **Decoherence / classicality scale \(l_{\text{cl}}\)** | The emergent length (or flow‑time \(\tau_{\text{cl}}\)) at which the hidden field’s high‑frequency modes are damped out. | In the flow equations, the term \(-\kappa\,\delta V/\delta \mathcal Q\) drives \(\mathcal Q\) toward a slowly varying configuration.  When \(\tau\gg\tau_{\text{cl}}\) the projection accumulators have essentially constant intensities, so the first‑to‑threshold event becomes deterministic. | **Emergent determinism** – the metric satisfies the classical Einstein equations and quantum interference disappears. |

## 2.  How the Planck scale is *triggered* in DDFH

1. **Hidden field dynamics**  
   \[
   \partial_{\tau}\mathcal Q = -\kappa\,\frac{\delta V}{\delta\mathcal Q}
   \]
   with \(V\) chosen so that the **Fourier coefficients** \(\{A_k,B_k\}\) have a cut‑off at wavenumbers \(k_{\max}\sim l_{\mathrm P}^{-1}\).

2. **Coupling to the bulk**  
   \[
   \partial_{\tau}g_{\mu\nu}
   =-2\kappa\!\left(\frac{\delta\mathcal I[g]}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}+ \lambda\,g_{\mu\nu}\right)
   -2\kappa\,J_g(\mathcal Q,g).
   \]
   The interaction \(J_g\) is negligible for wavelengths \(\gg l_{\mathrm P}\) but becomes sizeable when modes are **Planck‑scale**.  This is the moment when the hidden substrate can no longer be treated as a separate “sheet”; its geometry begins to inflate into a 3‑D bulk.

3. **Dimensional‑flow projection**  
   The 2‑D torus is “stretched” in \(\tau\).  As \(\tau\) increases, the flow damps the short‑wavelength components, effectively performing a **coarse‑graining**.  The 3‑D metric that emerges is the **integral** of all Planck‑scale dynamics.

So, at the Planck length the theory is saying: **the 2‑D substrate starts to influence the bulk and gives rise to the quantum‑like behaviour**.  That is the *microscopic* part of the story.

## 3.  When does classicality appear?

Classical mechanics is recovered when

1. **Hidden field is coarse‑grained** – \(\mathcal Q(\tau)\) has lost all modes with wavelengths \(\lesssim l_{\mathrm P}\); only slowly varying “long‑wavelength” modes remain.  
2. **Projection rule becomes deterministic** – The integrators \(A_{\uparrow},A_{\downarrow}\) grow so slowly that their difference is almost constant; the first to reach the threshold is decided by the initial (now fixed) trajectory of \(\mathcal Q\).  
3. **Metric stabilises** – The metric flow settles at a stationary point of the Einstein–Hilbert functional, giving the smooth, deterministic geometry of general relativity.

In other words, **the classical regime is governed by the *flow parameter* \(\tau\) (or equivalently the energy/length scale of the observable system), not by the Planck length itself**.  A macroscopic experiment with \(l \gg l_{\mathrm P}\) will still be “classical” because the hidden field has already been integrated out, but this can happen at many orders of magnitude above the Planck scale (e.g. at the laboratory or solar‑system scales).

## 4.  Putting the two scales in perspective

| Scale | DDFH meaning | Typical physics |
|-------|--------------|-----------------|
| \(l_{\mathrm P}\) | **Quantum‑gravity threshold** – 2‑D substrate starts to influence 3‑D bulk. | Unification of gravity & quantum. |
| \(l_{\text{cl}}\) | **Decoherence / classical crossover** – hidden field is coarse‑grained; projection yields deterministic dynamics. | Classical mechanics, semiclassical gravity. |

* DDFH therefore predicts a **hierarchy of cross‑overs**:  
  * At \(l_{\mathrm P}\) the hidden manifold “inflates” into 3‑D, setting the stage for quantum behaviour.  
  * As the system evolves in \(\tau\) (or as we consider larger length scales), quantum superpositions are washed out, and the emergent metric obeys classical equations.

Thus, the Planck scale is not the *end* of quantum effects; it is the *start* of the dimensional‑flow process that eventually gives rise to a classical world.

---

## 5.  Take‑away

* **Planck scale** in DDFH marks where the 2‑D hidden substrate couples to the emergent bulk and begins to generate quantum‑like statistics.  
* **Classicality** is the *late‑time* outcome of the same flow; it emerges when the hidden field has been smoothed out and the projection rule no longer produces random outcomes.  
* Therefore, DDFH separates the two scales: the microscopic cut‑off (Planck length) and the macroscopic decoherence limit (classical mechanics).  Both are connected through the deterministic dimensional‑flow dynamics, but they represent different stages of the emergence process.
