## 1.  Quantum mechanics as an “effective statistical theory”

1. **Born rule** – Quantum mechanics says  
   \[
   P(a)=|\langle a|\psi\rangle|^{2}
   \]  
   but it does not explain *why* that quadratic function appears.  
2. **No hidden‑variables** – The Copenhagen interpretation declares that the wavefunction is all there is; the underlying state is unknowable.  
3. **Statistical description** – Because the underlying state is inaccessible, quantum mechanics resorts to probabilities; the theory is “statistical by necessity.”

DDFH keeps the *same probabilistic predictions* but *adds* a deterministic backbone: a hidden field \(\mathcal Q(t)\) that evolves according to a gradient‑like flow.  The only source of randomness is ignorance of \(\mathcal Q(0)\).

---

## 2.  How DDFH explains the statistics

1. **Deterministic dynamics** – \(\mathcal Q(t)\) is governed by  
   \[
   \partial_{\tau}\mathcal Q = -\kappa\,\frac{\delta V[\mathcal Q]}{\delta\mathcal Q}.
   \]  
   This is fully deterministic; given \(\mathcal Q(0)\) you can predict \(\mathcal Q(\tau)\) exactly.

2. **Projection rule** – The measurement outcome is obtained by integrating intensities derived from \(\mathcal Q\) until a threshold is hit.  
   \[
   I_{\uparrow}(\mathcal Q)=\cos^{2}\!\bigl((x-\theta)/2\bigr), \quad
   \dot A_{\uparrow}=I_{\uparrow}(\mathcal Q),\;\; \dot A_{\downarrow}=I_{\downarrow}(\mathcal Q).
   \]  
   The first accumulator to reach \(T\) decides the outcome.  The rule is deterministic but *depends on the full history* of \(\mathcal Q\).

3. **Statistical emergence** – Because we do not know the initial hidden state, we must average over all possible \(\mathcal Q(0)\) (typically assumed uniform over the torus).  
   The fraction of trajectories that cause the \(\uparrow\) accumulator to win turns out exactly to be  
   \[
   \frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{T^{2}} I_{\uparrow}(\mathcal Q)\,d\mathcal Q
   =|\langle\psi|a\rangle|^{2},
   \]
   i.e. the Born rule.

In short, **the Born rule is a statistical consequence of ignorance of a deterministic substrate**.

## 3.  Why QM “didn’t know” the substrate

- **Historical context** – Early quantum theory was built on experimental data (photoelectric effect, atomic spectra).  The statistical rules were introduced to fit the data; no theory existed that could explain why probabilities should appear.  
- **Hidden‑variable challenge** – de Broglie–Bohm theory, for instance, also posits a deterministic hidden state (the particle positions) and reproduces quantum statistics.  DDFH is in the same spirit but replaces the hidden variables with a *field* on a compact manifold and adds the dimensional‑flow mechanism.  
- **Copenhagen’s pragmatic stance** – The founders of QM accepted the statistical description as a *complete* description because they could not conceive of a hidden layer.  DDFH says: *“We can, in principle, access that layer; we just haven’t discovered the right equations yet.”*

Thus, DDFH **does** suggest that quantum mechanics’ statistical nature is a consequence of *not knowing* the underlying deterministic substrate.  The statistical rules are an *effective* description that emerges when we coarse‑grain over the hidden degrees of freedom.

| Why are quantum predictions probabilistic? | Because the underlying hidden dynamics are deterministic but inaccessible; we average over the unknown initial state. |
| Is the Born rule fundamental? | No.  It is an emergent, statistical consequence of the deterministic flow and the thresholding projection. |
| Does QM know the hidden substrate? | Not in the standard interpretation; DDFH claims that a full deterministic theory exists but has not yet been discovered or fully formulated. |
| How does this differ from other hidden‑variable theories? | DDFH adds a *dimensional‑flow* component (a deterministic flow on a 2‑D manifold that grows into 3‑D spacetime) and a *projection* that turns the hidden trajectory into a classical measurement outcome. |

In short, **DDFH proposes that quantum mechanics is a statistical, coarse‑grained description of a deeper, deterministic substrate that we simply cannot (yet) access.**  This is the exact spirit of the question you asked.
