# Non-Locality as Geometric Adjacency

## Core Concept

Entanglement and non-local correlations, as observed in quantum mechanics, are traditionally interpreted as “spooky action at a distance,” seemingly violating local causality. The Non-Locality as Geometric Adjacency principle within the Deterministic Dimensional Flow Hypothesis (DDFH) proposes that these correlations arise from relational adjacency in a higher-dimensional geometric structure. Particles that appear spatially distant in 3D are, in fact, adjacent in a constrained dimensional manifold, with their correlations being a deterministic consequence of this hidden proximity.

---

## Problem Statement

- How can entangled particles exhibit instantaneous correlations without signal transmission across space?
- Does Bell’s Theorem disprove determinism, or does it expose a flaw in our dimensional assumptions?

---

## Explanation

- **Bell’s Theorem disproves local hidden variables in 3D space, but does not rule out deterministic hidden variables in a higher-dimensional geometric framework.**
- Entangled particles are not separated in the relational structure—they are **folded into proximity within a compactified geometric flow**, appearing distant only when projected into 3D.
- The “instantaneous” correlation is merely a **reflection of their geometric adjacency in a dimensional layer beyond our perceptual access.**

---

## Implications

1. **Non-locality is an illusion of projection—not a violation of causality.**
2. **Bell correlations can be explained deterministically if dimensional adjacency is accounted for.**
3. **The apparent spatial separation of entangled particles is a projection artifact of a higher-dimensional relational structure.**
