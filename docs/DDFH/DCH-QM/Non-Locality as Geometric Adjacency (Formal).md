
## Definition

The **Non-Locality as Geometric Adjacency** principle, within the Deterministic Dimensional Flow Hypothesis (DDFH), reinterprets quantum entanglement. What appears as “spooky action at a distance” in 3D space is explained as **relational adjacency in a constrained geometric substrate**. Entangled particles are not causally connected across space, but remain adjacent in a hidden dimensional manifold; their correlations are deterministic consequences of this adjacency.

---

## Core Concept

- Non-local correlations are projection artifacts of a higher-dimensional relational structure.
- Particles that appear spatially separated in 3D bulk remain adjacent in the substrate.
- Correlations reflect deterministic geometry, not violations of causality.

---

## Problem Statement

- How can entangled particles exhibit instantaneous correlations without superluminal signaling?
- Does Bell’s Theorem truly disprove determinism, or does it expose a limitation of assuming a purely 3D framework?

---

## Explanation

- **Bell’s Theorem:** disproves local hidden variables in 3D space, but not deterministic hidden variables in a higher-dimensional structure.
- Entangled states occupy a compactified relational fold where adjacency is preserved.
- “Instantaneous” correlations are reflections of this adjacency, not acausal influence.

---

## Implications

1. Non-locality is an illusion of projection; causality remains intact at the substrate.
2. Bell correlations can be explained deterministically once dimensional adjacency is accounted for.
3. Spatial separation of entangled particles is a projection artifact of higher-dimensional geometry.
4. Supports DDFH’s view that probabilistic QM is an epistemic shadow of hidden determinism.
