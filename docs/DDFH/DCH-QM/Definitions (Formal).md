# DDFH Definitions

This glossary provides precise definitions for key terms in the Deterministic Dimensional Flow Hypothesis (DDFH) and Genesis Scheme. It ensures a controlled vocabulary for building rigorous models and testable variants.

---

## 1. Ground

- **Definition:** The minimal necessary condition of existence, characterized by necessity, indivisibility, self-sufficiency, generativity, and lawfulness (per Ground Formula).
- **Role:** Logical prerequisite; not substance but condition.
- **Notation:** .

---

## 2. Flow

- **Definition:** A primitive relation in the Ground that can be infinitely divided, recombined, and interwoven.
- **Mathematical Candidate:** Directed relation, causal link, or algebraic morphism.
- **Notation:** .

---

## 3. Substrate

- **Definition:** A 2D informational manifold formed from interwoven flows. It encodes distinctions via area-based degrees of freedom.
- **Properties:**
    - Information scales with area, not volume.
    - Holds patterns that give rise to emergent bulk.
- **Notation:** .

---

## 4. Bulk

- **Definition:** The emergent 3D spacetime and objects, arising from the substrate’s informational patterns.
- **Properties:**
    - Degrees of freedom appear volumetric.
    - Scale, locality, and geometry are emergent.
- **Notation:** .

---

## 5. Emergence

- **Definition:** The lawful translation of substrate flows () into stable 3D bulk structures () when thresholds of relational density/organization are crossed.
- **Key Point:** No external projector—emergence is intrinsic to lawfulness of the Ground.

---

## 6. De-emergence

- **Definition:** The collapse of bulk degrees of freedom back into substrate encoding (), triggered by thresholds (e.g., excessive bulk density in black holes).
- **Key Point:** Preserves information, compresses volume into surface encoding.

---

## 7. Dimensional Compaction

- **Definition:** The epistemic/mathematical effect of describing substrate states () using bulk-based formalisms ().
- **Effect:** Deterministic 2D flows appear probabilistic/random when compacted into 3D math.
- **Example:** Quantum superposition = compaction artifact.

---

## 8. Quantum Paradox (DDFH sense)

- **Definition:** An apparent contradiction in bulk-level physics arising from misreading substrate determinism through compaction.
- **Resolution:** Bulk paradoxes are illusions of dimensional mismatch.

---

## Additional Terms:

## Dimensional Accessibility (α)

  
A scalar or function quantifying the effective degrees of freedom accessible at a given scale.
High α = unfolded, full bulk accessibility.
Low α = constrained, folded adjacency dominating.

Used in [[Dimensional Overfitting Hypothesis (Formal)]] and [[Scale-Dependent Dimensional Unfolding Hypothesis (Formal)]].
  

**Projection Artifact**
A stable regularity or law-like effect produced when deterministic lower-dimensional dynamics are expressed in higher dimensions.

Explains why probabilistic models (QM/QFT) exhibit extraordinary precision despite being epistemic abstractions.

Referenced in [[Precision Paradox Hypothesis (Formal)]].
