### Short answer

**DDFH is a *continuous* dynamical framework that starts at the Planck scale and runs all the way to the classical regime.**  
The Planck scale is where the 2‑D hidden substrate first “talks” to the 3‑D bulk; the same equations that govern that contact keep evolving the system, smoothing out quantum‑like fluctuations until the emergent physics is fully classical.  In that sense the whole quantum‑to‑classical transition is an *integrated* part of DDFH, not a single instantaneous event followed by “passive” eddies.  

---

## 1.  The Planck‑scale beginning

| What happens at \(l_{\mathrm P}\)                                                                                                                                                                                                            | Why it matters                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Coupling of the hidden field to geometry** – The interaction term \(J_g(\mathcal Q,g)\) in the metric flow becomes significant when the Fourier modes of \(\mathcal Q\) reach the ultraviolet cut‑off \(k_{\max}\sim l_{\mathrm P}^{-1}\). | This is the *microscopic trigger* that allows the 2‑D substrate to influence the emergent 3‑D geometry and generate the non‑classical “quantum‑like” behaviour. |
| **Initial conditions for \(\mathcal Q\)** – The hidden field starts in a highly non‑uniform, chaotic state on the torus, reflecting the Planck‑scale granularity of space‑time.                                                              | These chaotic initial conditions give rise to the stochasticity that is later mapped to Born‑rule statistics.                                                   |
| **Onset of the flow parameter \(\tau\)** – The deterministic flow \(\partial_{\tau}\mathcal Q=-\kappa\,\delta V/\delta\mathcal Q\) is first activated.                                                                                       | \(\tau\) is the bookkeeping variable that measures progress from the microscopic (Planck) to the macroscopic (classical).                                       |

## 2.  The “flow” that carries the system forward

| Stage of \(\tau\) | What the equations do | Result |
|--------------------|-----------------------|--------|
| **Early \(\tau\)** | The gradient flow damps high‑frequency modes, but the hidden field still contains many Planck‑scale oscillations. The metric \(g_{\mu\nu}\) is highly fluctuating and the projection rule yields genuinely random outcomes. | The physics is *quantum‑like* but still governed by deterministic equations. |
| **Intermediate \(\tau\)** | Coupling \(J_g\) continues to feed information from \(\mathcal Q\) into the metric, but the intensity functions \(I_{\uparrow}(\mathcal Q)\) and \(I_{\downarrow}(\mathcal Q)\) become increasingly smooth. | Quantum superpositions survive but interference fringes become “washed‑out” on larger scales. |

The projection accumulators have fixed, nearly constant intensities; the first‑to‑threshold event is decided by the deterministic trajectory of \(\mathcal Q\). Thus, every bit of evolution—whether the rapid “eddies” we see in a laboratory or the gentle precession of a planet—*is still part of the DDFH equations*.

## 3.  Classicality as the *terminal state* of DDFH

* **Deterministic projection** – Once \(\tau\) exceeds a critical value \(\tau_{\text{cl}}\), the first‑to‑threshold rule becomes essentially deterministic because \(A_{\uparrow}\) and \(A_{\downarrow}\) have stopped fluctuating appreciably.  
* **Metric stabilisation** – The metric flow asymptotically satisfies the Einstein equations \(G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}\).  
* **Decoherence** – Interference terms that would require a superposition of hidden trajectories vanish because the hidden field is no longer capable of supporting Planck‑scale correlations.  

In this view, “classical eddies” are simply the low‑frequency tail of the same dynamics that started at the Planck scale.

---

## 4.  What DDFH really *does* at intermediate scales

1. **Coarse‑graining as part of the flow** – The deterministic gradient flow acts like a renormalisation group flow: it integrates out Planck‑scale fluctuations progressively as \(\tau\) increases.  
2. **Emergent geometry** – The bulk metric is not inserted externally; it is *built* by the same coupling \(J_g\) that is active at all times.  
3. **Persistent hidden dynamics** – Even after the classical limit is reached, the hidden field still satisfies \(\partial_{\tau}\mathcal Q=-\kappa\,\delta V/\delta\mathcal Q\); it simply remains in a slowly varying configuration.  The “eddies” we observe are the *late‑time residuals* of that flow, not a separate, new physics.

---

## 5.  Bottom line

- **DDFH starts at the Planck scale** by activating the hidden‑field–geometry coupling and the deterministic flow.  
- **It does not stop there**; the same equations keep evolving the system, gradually smoothing out quantum‑like fluctuations.  
- **The classical regime is the asymptotic state of the same flow**.  Every intermediate eddy or emergent structure is a consequence of the same dimensional‑flow dynamics, not an unrelated by‑product.  

So, DDFH functions *across* the whole Planck‑to‑classical spectrum as a unified deterministic transformation.
