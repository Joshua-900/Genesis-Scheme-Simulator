# P-to-A Coupling Has a Measurable Field-Level Role

## Design

Twenty fixed seeds ran for 2,000 frames on the same high-compression V1-derived
substrate. Each seed was evaluated twice: V1 parity with all new couplings off,
then the existing independent-potential condition with potential transport,
source restoration, and P-to-A influence enabled. The predeclared observables
were P/A alignment, global actual-order, mean tension, mean compression, and
vortex count.

## Results

| Observable | V1 parity median | P-to-A median | Paired two-sided Wilcoxon `p` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| P/A alignment | 0.006 | 0.273 | 0.0000019 |
| Mean tension | 0.047 | 0.114 | 0.0000019 |
| Global actual-order | 0.176 | 0.198 | 0.498 |
| Mean compression | 0.066 | 0.068 | 0.985 |
| Vortex count | 35 | 38 | 0.034 |

## Interpretation

The active P-to-A coupling is not merely a passive annotation of V1: it
reliably produces P/A alignment, changes the retained-tension field, and shifts
vortex population in this regime. It does **not** yet establish a change in
global actual-order or mean compression, and it does not establish an
object-level advantage.

The earlier potential-only rotation result remains unresolved. That result
tested one local, 90-degree intervention on strict pair continuation; it is not
in tension with this field-level result.

## Next Test

Use the existing P-to-A bridge to test a sharper causal question: whether a
defined potential perturbation changes a field-level response beyond the
perturbed mask, with a matched background control. Do not add another coupling
until that propagation test is specified.
